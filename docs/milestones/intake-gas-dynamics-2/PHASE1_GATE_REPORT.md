## 4. Equations implemented
Per bank and step, evaluated once before the air-path root find, as before:

`VE_dyn = (η_ve(N, cams) · G_wave(N, T_man, stage) + scavenging) · float`

| Term | Implementation |
|---|---|
| η_ve (valve-event filling) | the existing parabola `VeCeiling · max(0.25, shape(N/N_ve))`, `N_ve = v·60/(2S)`, `v = v₀ + 0.15·(D − 220°) + 0.30·Δ_IVC` (guard ≥ 2 m/s); cam phase, VVL profile and centrelines act here only. **The runner factor `(0.300/L)^0.25` and `ReferenceRunnerLength` are removed.** |
| Stage geometry (built once) | `L_eff = L + δ·r_r`, `A_r = π·d²/4`, `V_eff = V_c + V_d/2` of the bank's own geometry, `β = A_r·L_eff/V_eff`, x the root of `x·tan x = β` on (0, π/2) by bisection |
| Tuned speed | `N_t = 60·x·a/(2π·L_eff) / (K·c₁)`, `c₁ = x(1) = 0.8603`, `a = √(γ·R·T_man)`; precomputed at 298.15 K, per step scaled by `a/a_ref` (exact) |
| T_man | the bank's manifold gas temperature **of the previous step** (the previous `AirPathResult`; ambient on the first step), so the gain stays out of the root find; exact on an NA engine |
| Response | `R(r) = 4ζ²r/((1 − r²)² + 4ζ²r²)`, `R̃(r) = R(r·r_p)/R(r_p)`, `r_p = √(((2 − c) + √((2 − c)² + 12))/6)`, `c = 4ζ²` (closed form of R's peak; 0.9389 at ζ = 0.35) |
| Amplitude | `A = min(A_max, κ·M_r)`, `M_r = ū_r/a`, `ū_r = V_d,cyl·(N/120)/(A_r·f_event)`, `f_event` the active profile's (duration@1 mm + 50°)/720 |
| Gain | `G_wave = 1 + A·R̃(N/N_t)` ∈ [1, 1 + A_max] by construction (A ∈ [0, A_max], R̃ ∈ [0, 1]); no cam-timing input |
| Stages | N stages; stage 0 is `runner_length_mm`; the ECU runs the highest stage whose switch speed is reached, keeping a stage down to its speed less 150 rpm |
| Scavenging, float, residuals, air-path solve | unchanged |

Cost per bank and step: one square root (the speed of sound) and one rational function; no new root find, no allocation.

Why the air moves the way it does (a documented generic correction): the runner's gain peaks near its tuned speed (the
340 mm OEM runner: 4,583 rpm at 298 K; G = 1.080 at 5,000 rpm, 1.004 at 1,500 rpm). The valve-event parabola cannot take
that shape, so the least-squares re-anchor lowers the low end (−2.5 %), keeps a bump at 4,500–4,750 (+2.9 %) and lowers
the top (−2.7 %) — as the proposal predicted ("reshapes filling by about half its amplitude, which v₀ and the ceiling
absorb only in part"). Torque follows: 147.9 → 143.8 N·m at 1,500 rpm, 189.0 → 193.2 at 4,000, 141.3 → 136.8 at 7,500.

**Margins and findings.**
- The physics level holds with 0.13 percentage points to spare. At the parameter band edges, with v₀ and the ceiling left
  at their nominal fit, it fails at K 2.2 (3.01 %), κ 0.2 (3.89 %) and κ 1.0 (8.58 %) (§8).
- The proposal expected the tune to move this metric by < 0.001 %. The full regeneration (VE cells up to 0.028) moved it
  by ≈ 0.06 percentage points (2.81 → 2.87 %), through charge cooling. Still ≈ 50 times under the tolerance, so the
  metric remains a physics check, but the stated independence is weaker than claimed.

The K20's other configurations are not anchored (R3). Their moves and causes are in §9.

Reference (published and dyno-thread shape): 300 N·m at 3,500 rpm, a dip near 4,000–4,200 rpm where DISA switches,
a second hump, 170 kW at 5,900 rpm (≈ 275 N·m). Bands (`M54ReferenceTests`, test 5): 270–330 N·m and 144.5–195.5 kW

What the generic model does and does not produce, asked as the authorization asked it:
- **Low and midrange filling.** Yes, in direction: the plateau from 1,500 rpm is gone, the curve rises 2.7 % to a
  mid-range peak at 3,100 rpm (reference 3,500). The rise is weak: VE_dyn rises 7 % from 1,500 to 4,000 rpm
  (0.98 → 1.05), but the port pressure falls 5 % over the same range (100.5 → 95.5 kPa, the estimated intake and port
  flows) and the residual factor 1.2 %, so trapped air (VE) rises only 1.6 % to 3,500 rpm.
- **The DISA stage transition.** Yes: the closed stage wins below its crossover (3,924 rpm, derived for 3,925) and the
  open stage above. The driver's switch speed, 3,900 rpm, lies in the sourced band, and the switched curve follows the
  upper envelope. The stage benefit is modest: +0.0 % at 1,000 and +1.0 % at 3,500 for the closed stage; +1.9 % at 4,500
  and +2.5 % at 5,000 for the open one. Against one fixed 380 mm runner, DISA adds 2.6 N·m at the peak (297.8 against 295.2 N·m).
- **The characteristic midrange behaviour.** Partly. There is a first hump. There is no marked dip at the switch
  (torque falls 297.0 → 290.6 N·m from 3,500 to 4,000 rpm, a continuing decline, not a dip and recovery) and **no second
  hump**. The top end stays 9.5 % short.
- **VANOS × runner.** The phaser keeps the valve-event filling at its ceiling at every speed (shape 0.99–1.00), and the
  runner gain now shapes the curve beneath it. The stage effect under the VANOS map equals the parked one exactly (test 4),
  because the intake-closing coupling is deferred (U6). The real engine's VANOS × DISA interaction through intake closing
  is therefore not represented.

**Classification of the miss** (no value was moved toward the reference):
| Miss | Class | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Peak at 3,100 instead of 3,500 rpm | B (documented limitation) | the closed stage's placement comes from A-D1 and the ±5 % K placement (K 2.0 → peak unchanged at 3,100; κ 1.0 → 3,500, §8); both are pre-registered |
| Weak rise from 1,500 rpm | B / A (content) | the gain is absorbed by the pressure drop across estimated flows (the investigation's E7) and κ is unsourced |
| No second hump, top end −9.5 % | B → D | needs the deferred group-plenum mode or pipe harmonics (DISA's real mechanism is a group resonance) and possibly the estimated flows; Phase 2 |

## 12. Tune regeneration and performance
**Tune regeneration.** Step 4 ran the driver over all 12 tunes (5.5 min). Eight were fixed points after one pass. Four
had cells that the driver's rule reported as unsettled: `k20.turbo_base` (1 VE cell), `syn.t20` (6 cam, 8 VE),
`syn.v6tt` (9 cam, 14 VE) and `syn.v8d` (1 cam). Two more passes on those settled them; the reason is flat cam optima on
the turbo maps. The M54 was regenerated again with DISA: its cam map was unchanged, the switch was set at 3,900 rpm and 42
VE cells moved. **All 12 tunes are fixed points** (`carsim regenerate-tunes`). The hand-authored spark audit went from 74
to 78 cells above the ceiling on the K20 stock (turbo base: 80, unchanged); the decision on them remains the owner's.
Notable table moves: `syn.r6` switch 6,400 → 4,700 rpm; `syn.t3` lift switch −200 rpm; `syn.v8d` cam cells up to 40.5°
(low-load, flat optima).

| `syn_v6_tt` | 159.6 µs, 28,032 B | 163.4 µs, 11,120 B | +2.4 % | +10 % |

How it got there (§15 item 7): the physics commit alone met the time budget (K20 ≈ 58–61 µs) but raised `syn_v6_tt`'s
allocation to 29,704 B, over the guard's 28,593 B. The cause was pre-existing: every air-path evaluation allocated
closures for its nested root finds, and `syn_v6_tt`'s flow solve took about two more iterations per bank at its new
operating point. Making those root finds allocation-free with a generic delegate solver then cost 40 µs per step under
.NET 8's dynamic PGO (97 µs; equal to the base with PGO or tiering off). Isolated by bisection, the trigger was the
downstream-pressure residual's three `Math.Pow` calls being inlined into the solver loop. The final form uses struct
functions, with that one residual kept out of line, and is bit-identical.

## 14. Failures, unexplained behaviour and open risks
1. **The K20 anchor's physics margin is thin** (2.87 % against 3 %) and fails at the band edges without a re-anchor
   (§8). Any later change touching filling will have to be checked against it carefully.
2. **The M54's top end and second hump are missing** (−9.5 % power). Classified B → D (§7). It is not a regression: the
   top end was −10 % before.
3. **The weak mid-range rise** comes from estimated flow content absorbing the gain. That is plausible, but it is not
   demonstrated against a measured M54.
4. **The PGO pathology** (§12) is explained by measurement, not by a JIT-level root cause. A future .NET or code change
   could reopen it; the benchmark and the allocation guard would show it.
5. **The knife-edge lap** of the worn project car flipped back to 0 laps. It is a diagnostic by design. It remains a
   reminder that the autopilot on a worn car is sensitive to sub-percent changes.
6. **Anchor independence from the tune** is weaker than stated (≈ 0.06 percentage points, not < 0.001 %; §6).
7. The sensitivity study ran in a scratch copy (parameters made static fields). It is described here (inputs: shipped
   content, frozen tunes, K20 anchor procedure, M54 sweeps 1,000–6,400 rpm at 100 rpm, 1 s settle, 298.15 K) but is not
   a repository tool.
8. Nothing is unexplained in the physics outputs. Every move in §9 has a stated cause.

## 15. Deviations from the proposal and clarifications
1. **R = 287.05 J/(kg·K)** (the project's air constant) instead of the table's 287. The open-stage tuned speed is
   4,208.5 rpm instead of 4,208; the derived closed stage is unchanged at 469.1 mm.
2. **Scavenging stays additive**: `VE_dyn = (η_ve·G + s)·float`, while §2.1 writes `η_ve·G·(1 + scavenging)·float`
   and says scavenging is unchanged. The code keeps today's term exactly. Stage crossovers are identical either way.
3. **N-stage schema names** (the proposal leaves them open): `switched_stages` (stage 0 is `runner_length_mm`), and the
   tune's `intake_runner_upper_switch_rpm` for stages 2 onwards.
4. **Commits.** R6's "one commit" holds for the physics, tunes and re-baseline. The DISA derivation was frozen in its own
   earlier commit (for provenance), and the DISA content, the tests-as-facts and the performance fix are separate
   commits.
5. **Test rigs** (no assertion or tolerance changed):
   - tests 1 and 4 pin their test-only 380 and 380/250 mm geometry explicitly, since the stock part now carries DISA;
   - test 5's relationship helpers skip points where the engine does not fire (its sweep ended in the rev limiter's fuel
     cut, where torque is motoring torque);
   - test 5's held-out report compares held speeds with a tolerance;
   - tests 1–3 print their measurements.
6. **Existing tests adapted, each documented in the file.** Deliberate re-pins:
   - the K20 dyno reference (`EngineAgnosticTests`);
   - the M54 model reference ranges (`M54ReferenceTests`).

   `TorqueCurveDiagnosisTests`: three inverted, one retired, four kept. Fixtures that followed the calibration:
   - `CamTimingTests` read the cam map's value instead of the hard-coded 35°;
   - `ChassisWearTests`' light-duty box is rated 350 instead of 360 N·m, restoring its stated ≈ 20 % overload after the
     T35 build lost 3.5 % at the limiter;
   - `EngineOutputTests`' short-runner test checks the runner's tuned speed, since the valve-event peak no longer moves
     with the runner.
7. **Outside the intake model:**
   - the allocation-free root finds (§12), needed for the allocation budget and bit-identical;
   - the tune driver's repeated passes until every tune is a fixed point;
   - the tools `carsim derive-stage` and `inspect`'s stage listing.
8. **Fit norm.** Least squares (the proposal did not name a norm); the values were rounded to the constants' precision.
9. **T_man of the previous step** is taken from the previous `AirPathResult`, not a new state field (initial value
   ambient, as specified).
10. **DISA failsafe.** The real flap is sprung open. In the model, a variable intake without ECU control stays on stage 0,
    which for the M54 is the closed stage. Documented in PARTS_DATABASE.md.
